Sania Verma

AI engineer working on vision-language models and agentic systems.

My work sits at the intersection of research and engineering. I recently completed my MSc in Artificial Intelligence at City St George's, University of London, where my thesis was carried out as a SPAARTA project with the Fleming Initiative at Imperial College London, Imperial College Healthcare NHS Trust and the health policy sciences team at City St George's. With a background in mechanical engineering and now AI, I bring an engineering mindset, creativity, and a research and development approach to my work.

I specialise in breaking complex problems into smaller components, designing a suitable approach for each, and joining them back together. I've demonstrated my technical findings through collaborative research with industry (EPAM Systems, UK; MathWorks, USA) and academia (University of Hull, City St George's, and Dayalbagh Educational Institute, India). Earlier, I worked as a research intern at the Murty Lab at Georgia Tech and at Peking University, China.

I've shared my technical work with people from varied backgrounds at national and international conferences, including the 2024 IEEE International Conference on Signal, Information and Data Processing (ICSIDP) in Zhuhai, China (November 2024); the 2nd International Conference on Mind, Brain and Consciousness (MBCC 2025) at IIT Mandi, India (June 2025); NLDB 2026, the 31st International Conference on Natural Language & Information Systems, in Trondheim, Norway; and the GLOW workshop at ISWC 2026 in Bari, Italy.

I've also worked in industry, applying my academic research skills to deployment and to building real-world, client-focused projects. Currently, I'm an AI Engineer Intern at Noos AI, building LLM-powered agentic workflows for robotic inspection, including vision-language-action approaches to wind turbine hub-bolt inspection with LiDAR and vision. Together with Noos AI's co-founders, I secured an allocation of 5,000 GPU hours on LUMI-G through a successful EuroHPC compute application, to develop frugal edge AI models for robotic control in wind turbine inspection.

Explainability Why language models predict what they predict

    Collaboration: EPAM Systems, UK; University of Hull, UK
